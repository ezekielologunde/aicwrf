# AI-Integrated Cybersecurity Workforce-Readiness Framework (AICWRF)
**Version 0.2, 2026-11-27. Author: Ezekiel Ologunde. Self-published. Not peer reviewed. Not adopted by any institution. Not sponsored or endorsed by DoD, NIST, NICE, any military branch, or any agency.**

## 1. Purpose and scope
A documented, reusable way for community college and career-technical educators to teach core cybersecurity skills together with AI-security skills, with every module aligned to NICE Framework work roles and to industry certifications. It has two tracks that share one common core:

- **Common core (verify and document):** select evidence, draft, verify, report. First unit: a 50-minute lesson on verifying AI-generated security claims against synthetic evidence (in preparation, not part of this release).
- **Track A, AI literacy** (general digital and technology courses): verifying AI claims, protecting sensitive information. Proposed, not built.
- **Track B, AI security** (cybersecurity courses): defensive uses of AI, evaluating AI-system safeguards, adversarial ML awareness, secure use of AI systems, OT/IoT where appropriate. Proposed, not built.

Instructors choose modules and depth by prerequisites and goals.

**Audience:** instructors (primary); learners, including military-affiliated learners and emerging professionals; program advisory committees.
**Not claimed:** job readiness, DoD 8140 qualification, accreditation, endorsement, causation, or adoption.

## 2. Learning outcomes (draft)
After a module sequence a learner can (1) apply core defensive practice; (2) use AI tools on security work and verify the output against real data; (3) explain how attackers use AI and how to defend against it; (4) describe adversarial ML and AI data-security risks; (5) document findings professionally; (6) map own skills to a named work role.

## 5. Performance assessment
AI-module rubric: documented AI exchange; at least one AI claim verified and corrected against real data; accurate final write-up. The planned verification lesson adds a four-dimension 0 to 2 rubric (traceability, correction, uncertainty, handoff). A work-role task rubric per module is scored against the confirmed task statements.

## 7. Evaluation (planned)
Aggregate and de-identified data only. Pre/post within a course where terms allow; small-sample limits reported; no causal claims; null and negative results reported. Learner data leaves a site only with that site's privacy and research-review approval. No military status is collected.
